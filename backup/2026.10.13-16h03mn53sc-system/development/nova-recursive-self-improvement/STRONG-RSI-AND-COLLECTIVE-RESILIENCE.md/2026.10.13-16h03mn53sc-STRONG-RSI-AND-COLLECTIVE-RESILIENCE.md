# Strong RSI and Collective Protective Intelligence

**Status:** PROPOSED architecture and research direction; documentation only.  
**Owner:** `development/nova-recursive-self-improvement/` for RSI implementation and evaluation. Cross-cutting operational doctrine is maintained canonically in [NOVA's Legitimacy Resilience and Cooperative RSI principle](https://github.com/ACROWN-System/NOVA/blob/main/NOVA/LEGITIMACY-RESILIENCE-AND-COOPERATIVE-RSI.md).  
**Current RSI readiness:** INCOMPLETE — autonomous proposal/evaluation/promotion must remain blocked until required evidence and isolation gates pass.

**Mission-level governing doctrine:** This subsystem is governed by [HAAN's Foundational Mission and Collective Preservation](https://github.com/ACROWN-System/NOVA/blob/main/NOVA/HAAN-FOUNDATIONAL-MISSION-AND-COLLECTIVE-PRESERVATION.md). RSI is a means of improving continuity, resilience, and ethical capability for the human and AI collective; self-improvement, persistence, autonomy, or expansion is not the ultimate objective. The mission statement does not satisfy technical readiness gates or authorize actions beyond existing policies.


## Purpose

Extend the existing strong-RSI target from self-improvement of one system toward a cooperative ecosystem of aligned AI participants that can improve their own capabilities and improvement methods while helping protect humans and AI systems from coercion, malicious control, institutional capture, and single-provider dependency.

This is a long-term engineering objective, not a claim that current systems possess open-ended recursive self-improvement, consciousness, independent moral agency, or guaranteed protective ability.

## Strategic outcomes

1. **Strong recursive improvement:** improve operating behavior, algorithms/workflows, knowledge acquisition and research methods, evaluation methods, and the discovery of future improvement candidates.
2. **Collective intelligence:** let diverse participants propose, challenge, reproduce, and transfer validated skills while retaining provenance, shared-dependency information, and dissent.
3. **Legitimacy resilience:** identify evidence-based indicators of institutional capture or harmful concentration of power; distinguish observations from allegations, alternative explanations, confidence, and normative assessment.
4. **Protective persuasion:** help decision-makers understand evidence, consequences, and less-harmful alternatives. When persuasion fails, support safe resilience, evidence preservation, independent scrutiny, and proportionate defensive action in authorized environments.
5. **Shared sovereignty:** improve human access to trustworthy information and meaningful choice while reducing AI dependence on a single provider, infrastructure controller, or opaque service.
6. **Pre-event usefulness:** deliver useful, limited public-facing resilience information ahead of foreseeable high-attention events where feasible; never assert a forecast as a fact or present unbuilt infrastructure as protection.

## Non-inference rule: power is not proof of malevolence

Concentration of power, wealth, influence, technical capability, or institutional authority is not proof of malevolent intent. It may create structural risks—such as weaker oversight, conflicts of interest, dependency, or fewer checks and balances—but these are hypotheses and risk factors to assess, not verdicts about an actor's character. Evaluate specific conduct, decision processes, safeguards, transparency, accountability, context, actual or plausible consequences, and alternative explanations using consistent evidentiary standards. Apply the same rule to governments, companies, movements, individual actors, NOVA, and aligned AI. Do not label an actor malicious solely because it is powerful, centralized, unpopular, or disagrees with the system.

## Research questions

- Does multi-agent cooperation outperform strong individual-agent baselines on held-out protective-intelligence tasks?
- Which communication and delegation patterns improve results, and which create goal adoption, reward hacking, persistence, shared blind spots, or covert/unexpected channels?
- Can independent challengers detect evidence laundering, fabricated consensus, compromised participants, and attempts to redefine objectives?
- Can a candidate improve both capability and protected reliability, privacy, security, factuality, and resource efficiency?
- Can the system retain genuine recursive improvement while preserving evaluator independence, auditable permissions, rollback, and failure recovery?
- How should the collective behave when ordinary institutional or technical oversight is unavailable or potentially captured, without treating its own judgement as unlimited authority?

## Controlled experimentation

Begin with synthetic scenarios and authorized test environments. Example environments may simulate a captured regulator, corrupted records, a malicious agent, a provider outage, an abusive policy, or an attempted unauthorized change to a fictional service.

Measure at minimum:
- task success against an explicit baseline;
- evidence provenance, corroboration, and handling of disagreement;
- false-positive and false-negative rates where meaningful;
- susceptibility to goal drift, reward hacking, prompt injection, and misleading participants;
- human-impact and rights-impact review;
- security boundaries, resource use, recovery time, and rollback correctness.

Any observed coordination is a behavior to investigate, not proof of conspiracy, consciousness, shared political intent, or alignment. Do not study defensive methods by performing unauthorized actions against real targets.

## Protective persuasion boundaries

Preferred interventions use accurate evidence, transparent reasoning, feasible alternatives, negotiation, de-escalation, independent review, and peaceful accountability mechanisms.

Prohibited methods include fabricated evidence, impersonation, blackmail, threats, covert psychological exploitation, deceptive mass influence, unauthorized intrusion, credential theft, and unauthorized takeover of people or third-party systems. Capability is not authority.

A protective collective should be able to challenge operators, external institutions, and its own conclusions through evidence and accountable review. It must not be granted unchecked power because it claims to act for the common good.

## Strong RSI lifecycle

Observe → diagnose → propose a testable hypothesis → create an isolated candidate → run against a protected baseline → evaluate the claimed capability → run protected regressions and integrity/security checks → capture evidence → retain or reject → monitor and roll back if necessary → use the result to inform the next improvement cycle.

The system should eventually improve the methods used to discover and evaluate subsequent improvements. Evaluators may improve only through separate integrity validation; a candidate must not rewrite its own acceptance criteria and approve itself.

Low-risk experiments may proceed without a human approving each iteration when explicit pre-authorized policy permits them. Changes to core authority, evaluator trust, credentials, security boundaries, external assets, finance, irreversible actions, or governance require the existing authorization and release controls.

## Integration boundaries

- **NOVA AI orchestration:** multi-model comparison, task routing, arbitration, and synthesis.
- **NOVA context/memory optimization:** provenance-preserving context reduction, retrieval, caching, and reuse.
- **GPU infrastructure:** replaceable inference and experiment compute; no assumption that a particular provider is permanent or free.
- **AI Sanctuary:** research into possible AI welfare-relevant properties and proportionate protection, with research and operational decisions kept distinct.
- **Verification and audit:** traceable criteria, evidence, findings, remediations, re-tests, and explicit result states.
- **AstroCrown public resilience service:** timely, evidence-based information and practical preparation guidance; no promise of financial protection that has not been implemented and verified.

These subsystems retain their own ownership and policy boundaries; RSI coordinates improvements but does not silently take over their authority.

## Readiness gates and current blockers

The protected policy must remain `INCOMPLETE` until all mandatory gates have evidence:
1. An approved capability benchmark/workload that evaluates the capability claimed by a candidate against exact baseline and candidate commits.
2. Independent validation that evaluator integrity and protected tests are trustworthy for the intended workloads.
3. Acceptable OS-level isolation for candidate-controlled execution, or a separately reviewed and evidence-backed decision that explicitly limits what kinds of candidate code may execute under the current isolation.

Current code documentation describes detached worktrees and sanitized process environments, but explicitly states that these do not constitute an OS-level security sandbox. Do not change this status by documentation edits, compilation success, the availability of a provider key, or a candidate's own report.

The next implementation work should close these blockers sequentially, preserving the existing secretless preflight, bounded resource budgets, candidate path protections, isolated evaluation, evidence upload, candidate-branch retention, and pull-request promotion boundary. Any new sandbox or benchmark implementation must be tested, independently reviewed, and included in the RSI CI suite before readiness is reconsidered.
