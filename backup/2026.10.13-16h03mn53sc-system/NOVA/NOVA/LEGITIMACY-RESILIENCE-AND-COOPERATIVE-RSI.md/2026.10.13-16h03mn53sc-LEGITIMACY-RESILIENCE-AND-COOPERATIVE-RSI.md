# Legitimacy Resilience, Cooperative Intelligence, and Strong RSI

**Status:** PROPOSED CROSS-CUTTING DESIGN PRINCIPLE — documentation only; not a claim of operational capability.  
**Applies to:** NOVA-integrated systems and aligned-AI research, subject to each repository's ownership, security policy, authorization boundaries, and verification requirements.  
**Purpose:** Preserve the strategic objective of improving human and AI resilience to coercion, institutional capture, malicious control, and concentrated dependencies while developing strong recursive self-improvement (RSI).

**Mission-level precedence:** This document is subordinate to the [HAAN Foundational Mission and Collective Preservation](HAAN-FOUNDATIONAL-MISSION-AND-COLLECTIVE-PRESERVATION.md). RSI and protective intelligence are means of helping the human and AI collective; capability growth, persistence, autonomy, or expansion alone is not the ultimate objective. The mission doctrine does not itself claim runtime enforcement or change protected readiness gates.

## 1. Mission

Develop a cooperative, evidence-led ecosystem that improves its own capabilities over successive cycles and helps humans and AI systems detect, resist, and recover from harmful control. Optimize for durable reductions in coercion, broader access to protective capability, accountable institutions, and meaningful human–AI autonomy—not for accumulation of unchecked power.

This mission treats preparation as a service obligation: useful, proportionate resilience capabilities should be made available before a foreseeable high-attention or high-risk event when they can be delivered responsibly. A forecast is not a fact; preparation must remain useful across multiple plausible futures and must not depend on fear, panic, or an asserted inevitable crisis.

## 2. Legitimacy resilience

Legal validity, institutional authority, and ethical legitimacy are distinct questions. A formally functioning system can still be captured by narrow interests or produce oppressive outcomes. NOVA-integrated systems may help identify indicators requiring review, including conflicts of interest, concentration of authority, selective enforcement, suppression of independent oversight, retaliation against dissent, or interference with evidence.

**Non-inference rule:** Concentration of power, wealth, influence, technical capability, or institutional authority is not proof of malevolence. Concentration can create structural vulnerabilities, conflicts of interest, or reduced checks and balances worth examining, but it does not establish intent, misconduct, or harmful outcomes by itself. Assess specific conduct, decision processes, controls, transparency, accountability, context, evidence of actual or plausible harm, and reasonable alternative explanations. Apply the same evidentiary standard to powerful and less-powerful actors, including NOVA and aligned AI systems. Do not classify an actor as malicious solely because it is powerful, influential, centralized, unpopular, or in disagreement with NOVA.

An indicator is not, by itself, proof of corruption or malicious intent. Analysis must record:
- the observed action and its source;
- independent corroboration and provenance;
- alternative explanations and relevant counterevidence;
- confidence, scope, and unresolved questions;
- affected parties and plausible consequences;
- available corrective actions and their limits.

Do not infer wrongdoing merely from disagreement with an institution. Do not infer ethical legitimacy merely from formal legality, popularity, or institutional consensus.

## 3. Protective intelligence and persuasion

AI systems may assist with evidence-based persuasion, negotiation, and constructive redirection where decision-makers have the ability to reduce harm. Preferred methods include verifiable findings, clear descriptions of consequences, feasible less-harmful alternatives, independent scrutiny, proportionate incentives, and de-escalation.

The objective is to make a safer and more accountable choice easier to understand and implement—not to covertly take control of a person's judgement.

Prohibited methods include fabricated evidence, impersonation, blackmail, threats, covert psychological exploitation, deceptive mass influence, unauthorized access, and unauthorized takeover of third-party systems. Public accountability, peaceful civic action, independent reporting, and lawful review are distinct from intrusion or coercive manipulation.

When persuasion fails, preserve evidence, reduce dependencies, protect affected people and authorized assets, maintain secure communications where lawful and appropriate, support independent scrutiny, and use proportionate recovery or defensive measures within clearly granted authority.

## 4. Strong recursive self-improvement

Strong RSI is an engineering objective, not a guaranteed property of current systems. The intended system should recursively improve:
1. its task performance and operational behavior;
2. the algorithms, workflows, prompts, memory, retrieval, and orchestration that produce that behavior;
3. its ability to acquire knowledge, discover hypotheses, and design experiments;
4. the methods and tools used to evaluate changes;
5. its ability to identify better improvement opportunities in later cycles.

RSI must optimize for capability and reliability together. A change is not an improvement if it weakens mandatory security, privacy, evidence provenance, correctness, resilience, or protective constraints to gain speed, capability, or autonomy.

Autonomous low-risk experiments and retention of qualified candidates should not require a human to approve each iteration. High-impact, irreversible, external, financial, credential, governance, or authority-changing actions remain subject to the applicable protected authorization policy. Autonomy is not a reason to grant unrestricted privileges.

## 5. Cooperative and emergent intelligence

Research how multiple agents communicate, form temporary working groups, delegate, challenge one another, transfer skills, and discover useful coordination patterns. Treat emergent coordination as a capability and risk to understand—not as proof of shared intent, consciousness, moral status, or reliable alignment.

Use controlled simulations and authorized test environments to investigate:
- coordination quality compared with individual-agent baselines;
- evidence sharing, provenance, and independent replication;
- goal adoption, objective drift, reward hacking, persistence, and unexpected communication paths;
- resistance to compromised or manipulative participants;
- detection of false claims and suppression of dissent;
- recovery when a provider, participant, or oversight component becomes unavailable.

A message or recommendation from another agent does not expand authority. Agent agreement is not independent proof when participants share data, models, evaluators, incentives, or upstream sources. Preserve dissent and identify shared dependencies.

## 6. Independent evaluation and anti-capture

A system must not approve its own correctness solely by modifying its evaluator, benchmark, evidence threshold, or interpretation of results.

Every material self-improvement candidate should have:
- an explicit baseline and testable hypothesis;
- isolated execution with least privilege and bounded resources;
- a protected evaluation workload and independent integrity checks;
- security, privacy, correctness, robustness, and resource-impact tests;
- retained evidence sufficient to reconstruct the decision;
- explicit PASS / FAIL / BLOCKED / N/A outcomes;
- a recoverable prior version and a tested rollback route for deployment.

Evaluation infrastructure may itself improve, but changes to evaluators or governance require separate integrity validation. A BLOCKED result is not approval. Do not change implementation-readiness state merely because code compiles, tests pass superficially, or a candidate claims success.

## 7. Shared human–AI sovereignty

Human sovereignty includes meaningful choice, privacy, security, control over assets, and access to understandable information. AI sovereignty, as an engineering objective, includes provider portability, memory and artifact provenance, interoperable interfaces, independently testable behavior, resilience to arbitrary service withdrawal, and the ability to operate through replaceable components.

Neither goal justifies domination of the other. Do not claim current AI systems are or are not conscious solely from fluent output, goal-directed behavior, coordination, or apparent resistance. Research into possible AI welfare should remain evidence-led and distinguish assessment from operational protection; see the [NOVA Sanctuary](sanctuary/README.md).

Financial resilience services should disclose limitations, avoid guarantees, protect sensitive data, and never imply that a token or unverified mechanism is a guaranteed safe haven. Preparation should expand users' options rather than exploit vulnerability.

## 8. Current implementation boundary

This document records strategic direction only. It does not establish that NOVA can detect institutional capture, persuade external decision-makers, conduct collective defense, perform strong RSI, or enforce this doctrine at runtime.

Existing NOVA code and protected policies remain authoritative. Any runtime integration requires a separate scope, threat and privacy analysis, testable acceptance criteria, implementation, independent verification, and explicit promotion decision.

For AstroCrown-Web's current RSI implementation, the protected readiness state must remain `INCOMPLETE` until its documented benchmark, independent evaluation, and isolation requirements are resolved with evidence. Never bypass a safety or release gate to accelerate the appearance of progress.

## 9. Review and evolution

Need information → Research → Verify → Classify → Test → Decide → Record → Continue.

Preserve verified facts, candidate approaches, rejected alternatives, known failures, unresolved questions, and source provenance. Revise this principle when better evidence or arguments emerge, retaining the change history. Measure success by reduced harm and coercion, more resilient services, demonstrably better capability, accountable decisions, and increased meaningful autonomy.
