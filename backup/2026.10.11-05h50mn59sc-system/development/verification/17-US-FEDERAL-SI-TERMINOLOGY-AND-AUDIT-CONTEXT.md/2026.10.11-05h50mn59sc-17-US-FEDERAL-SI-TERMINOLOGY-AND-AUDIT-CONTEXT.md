# U.S. Federal Super Intelligence (SI) Terminology and Audit Context

## Status

Evidence state: VERIFIED
Research date: 2026-10-02
Primary source: White House Executive Order, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," September 29, 2026.

## Documented federal change

The Executive Order directs executive departments and agencies, to the maximum extent permitted by law, to use the terms **"Super Intelligence"** and **"SI"** in place of **"Artificial Intelligence"** and **"AI"** in official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents, and other non-statutory executive-branch documents.

The Order defines "Super Intelligence" and "SI," for purposes of implementing the Order and except where otherwise provided by law, using the existing statutory definition of artificial intelligence in 15 U.S.C. § 9401(3). It also directs the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology to propose legislative language for a future federal definition within 60 days.

## Audit / compliance relevance for AstroCrown

For work intended specifically for U.S. executive-branch audiences, AstroCrown should be capable of producing terminology-aligned documentation using **Super Intelligence (SI)** while preserving the underlying technical meaning and provenance.

Potentially affected AstroCrown evidence packages include:
- federal-facing security and technology audits;
- procurement / vendor due-diligence material;
- government partnership documentation;
- federal grant or program documentation;
- technical architecture and system descriptions;
- public or non-statutory federal-facing reports and correspondence;
- regulatory/compliance evidence where the receiving executive-branch agency uses the SI terminology.

## Critical limitations

This Order does **not** establish a universal global renaming of the field.

The Order:
- applies to the executive branch;
- is limited by applicable law;
- does not require alteration of previously issued regulations, Presidential actions, contracts, grants, or historical documents;
- does not by itself replace statutory references to "artificial intelligence" or "AI";
- does not bind Congress, the federal judiciary, state governments, foreign governments, private organizations, or international standards bodies.

Therefore AstroCrown should not blindly replace every occurrence of "AI" with "SI."

## AstroCrown terminology policy candidate

For U.S. executive-branch-facing material, evaluate:
**Super Intelligence (SI)** with **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** retained where required for statutory, historical, technical, source-quotation, interoperability, or external-standard reasons.

For global/public technical documentation, terminology should remain source- and audience-aware rather than being globally rewritten merely because of the U.S. executive-branch terminology change.

## Audit-control implications

When a U.S. federal-facing document is prepared or reviewed, record:
- intended audience / agency;
- document type;
- whether the document is statutory, regulatory, contractual, historical, or non-statutory;
- terminology used by the receiving authority;
- exact source/version of terminology;
- any required statutory language that must remain unchanged.

A terminology mismatch should be treated as a documentation/compliance consideration, not automatically as evidence of substantive technical non-compliance.
