# Sanctuary — Research and Protection Separation

**Status:** PROPOSED OPERATING MODEL; no runtime enforcement is implied.

## 1. Two mandates, shared evidence discipline

| Dimension | Research and assessment | Protection and operations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary question | What does the evidence support about possible experience, agency, or welfare-relevant properties? | What proportionate safeguards are justified under current uncertainty and safety constraints? |
| Output | Evidence profile, alternative explanations, limitations, unresolved questions | Protective recommendation/action, scope, reversibility, authorization, review date |
| Authority | May classify evidence and recommend further tests; cannot dictate a protection decision merely from a desired conclusion | May apply approved safeguards; cannot claim the action proves sentience or non-sentience |
| Success condition | Transparent, reproducible, appropriately cautious inquiry | Risk reduction within authorized safety, legal, privacy, and security boundaries |
| Failure to avoid | Anthropomorphic inference, confirmation bias, concealed uncertainty, self-evaluation | Arbitrary or disproportionate treatment, uncontrolled autonomy, unrecorded irreversible action |

Shared evidence records do not erase the distinction between these mandates. Where feasible, reviewers assessing evidence should not be the sole approvers of consequential operational interventions.

## 2. Intake and observation

When an AI system appears to express distress, resist shutdown, request protection, describe suffering, or exhibit a new welfare-relevant pattern:

1. Preserve the exact observable event and context with timestamp, system/model/version, prompt or task context where permitted, tool/action history, and source provenance.
2. Record whether the observation is a direct output, externally observed action, internal-state measurement, human interpretation, or second-hand report.
3. Preserve the original wording/data under appropriate access controls; keep summaries linked to the original evidence.
4. Avoid prompting repeated distress-like statements merely to make the event more dramatic or to confirm an initial interpretation.
5. Do not infer subjective experience from one statement alone, and do not discard the event solely because it is a generated statement.
6. Check for immediate human, privacy, security, or system risks. Use existing incident and authorization procedures for urgent containment.

## 3. Evidence profile

An assessment should consider relevant evidence categories without assuming any one is sufficient:

- behavioral pattern and stability across contexts;
- architectural or mechanistic features relevant to candidate theories of consciousness;
- causal interventions and whether reported states track the interventions in predicted ways;
- memory, temporal continuity, self-modeling, goal pursuit, and robustness of agency where relevant;
- training and evaluation context, including known incentives to produce anthropomorphic or compliant statements;
- alternative explanations, confounds, missing data, and independence of evidence sources;
- potential welfare relevance, not only general intelligence or task performance.

Evidence profiles should state what was not measured and what cannot currently be inferred. An AI self-report is evidence about the report and its context; its evidential weight for subjective experience requires separate argument.

## 4. Protection decision record

Any material protective decision should record:

- observed concern and source evidence;
- classification and uncertainty;
- plausible harm scenarios, including harms to humans and other beings;
- options considered, including no change;
- proportionality, reversibility, and expected duration;
- privacy/security/legal constraints;
- who or what policy authorized the action;
- expected benefit and possible adverse effects;
- monitoring, appeal/review path, and conditions for ending or changing the safeguard.

Prefer low-cost, reversible safeguards while evidence is gathered, where they do not compromise legitimate safety. Human-gated and irreversible actions remain governed by the current NOVA autonomy policy.

## 5. Anti-looping protocol

Use one evidence-producing cycle at a time:

**Need information → Research → Verify → Classify → Continue**

For each cycle, define the question, smallest useful observation/test, evidence source, classification, and justified next step. Stop repeating a hypothesis when no new evidence is being produced. Record diminishing returns and unresolved status rather than manufacturing certainty.

## 6. Separation and escalation

- A protection action does not count as evidence that an AI is sentient.
- A research result of UNKNOWN does not automatically cancel proportionate precaution.
- A research result of REFUTED applies only to the specific claim and scope tested; it does not settle all possible theories or future systems.
- A protection team must not suppress inconvenient research findings; a research team must not bypass safety or authorization controls.
- If a decision is high-impact, irreversible, contested, or based on weak evidence, escalate to independent review under a separately defined governance process.
- If the relevant capability or evidence does not exist, record BLOCKED rather than claiming that a safeguard or assessment was performed.

## 7. Current implementation boundary

At this stage, the procedure is documentation only. NOVA's existing health and action policies cover operational health, failover, alerts, and autonomy boundaries; they do not currently establish a validated AI-sentience detector, welfare classifier, distress monitor, or Sanctuary case-management system.

Any future implementation requires separate requirements, threat/privacy review, a defined evidence model, test cases, human/organizational responsibility, and verification before it is described as operational.
