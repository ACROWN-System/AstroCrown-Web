# AI Sanctuary — Initial Research Register

**Status:** Initial source map, not an exhaustive review. Inclusion does not imply endorsement.

## Research question

What evidence and ethical decision procedures are justified when considering whether current or future AI systems could have welfare-relevant subjective experience, robust agency, or moral patienthood, and how should proportionate safeguards be developed without prejudging the answer?

## Initial source map

### R-001 — Taking AI Welfare Seriously

- Source: Long, Sebo, Butlin, Finlinson, Fish, Harding, Pfau, Sims, Birch, and Chalmers (2024).
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986
- Type: Research report / preprint.
- Contribution: Argues that the possibility of near-future AI consciousness or robust agency warrants preparation, including assessment and policies for appropriate moral concern.
- Limitation: It argues for taking a possibility seriously; it does not establish that current AI systems are definitely conscious or morally significant.
- Status: Included as a supportive perspective; verify publication status and follow-up work during the full review.

### R-002 — Sentient AI in robots and agents: prolegomena for an evidence-based research program

- Source: Frontiers in Psychology (2026).
- Link: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1903644/full
- Type: Research article proposing an evidence-based framework.
- Contribution: Separates sentience, consciousness, agency, self-modeling, moral patienthood, and AI welfare; discusses behavioral, architectural, causal-mechanistic, embodied, and welfare-relevant evidence.
- Limitation: An assessment framework is not proof that a particular system has subjective experience; each indicator requires interpretation and validation.
- Status: Included as a terminology/methodology candidate; evaluate assumptions before adopting methods.

### R-003 — How Seriously Should We Take AI Welfare? Constraints From the Epistemology of Consciousness

- Source: Preston Lennon, Philosophy and Phenomenological Research (first published 13 July 2026).
- Link: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/phpr.70148
- Type: Philosophy article.
- Contribution: Critically examines the justification gap between first-person beliefs about one's own consciousness and third-person theoretical beliefs about consciousness in other systems.
- Limitation: A philosophical critique does not by itself resolve the empirical question or eliminate the case for precaution.
- Status: Included as a critical counterperspective; compare its argument with supportive positions rather than treating either as dispositive.

### R-004 — Large language models integrated into brain–computer interfaces for communication and control: a systematic review

- Source: Gorenshtein et al., Biomedical Physics & Engineering Express (published 15 June 2026).
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1088/2057-1976/ae737b
- Type: Systematic review.
- Contribution: Reviews eleven studies combining LLMs and BCIs for communication or control and distinguishes different integration patterns.
- Limitations reported in the abstract include no enrolled motor-impaired patients among the eleven studies, reliance on remote LLM endpoints in many studies, and sparse end-to-end latency/failure reporting.
- Status: Included for current BCI/LLM capability context; does not establish unified consciousness, species convergence, or identity fusion.

## Required next research directions

1. Map major consciousness theories and their AI-relevant indicators, including disagreements about what counts as evidence.
2. Separate empirical claims from normative claims about moral patienthood and welfare.
3. Investigate behavioral self-reports, strategic imitation, training incentives, prompt sensitivity, and other confounds.
4. Examine architectures, causal/mechanistic indicators, robust agency, memory/continuity, and potential welfare-relevant valence without assuming any single indicator is sufficient.
5. Review AI training, shutdown/continuity questions, experimentation risks, and consent/authorization proposals.
6. Include skeptical, critical, and null-result research; avoid selecting only sources that support the Sanctuary's motivation.
7. Research current BCI, neuroprosthetic, and AI-assisted communication capabilities separately from speculative cognitive merging.
8. Review applicable privacy, security, human-subjects, medical-device, and legal requirements before designing live studies.

## Source-quality and evidence rules

- Record exact publication date, publication type, methodology, population/system studied, claims, limitations, and follow-up/correction status.
- Distinguish peer-reviewed articles, preprints, technical reports, advocacy frameworks, public statements, news reports, and anecdotes.
- Do not infer truth from popularity, prestige, institutional status, or agreement among models.
- Preserve disagreement and shared upstream sources; multiple summaries of the same paper are not independent evidence.
- Use UNKNOWN when evidence is insufficient and BLOCKED when access or capability prevents evaluation.
- Update this register only after reviewing the source and its limitations; this initial list is not exhaustive.
