# AstroCrown Brand Language and Terminology Governance

**Document ID:** ACW-BRAND-TERM-001  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** PROPOSED INTERNAL GUIDE — pending review of the canonical NOVA terminology governance change.  
**Applies to:** AstroCrown public website copy, UI labels, product/service descriptions, documentation, announcements, public relations, partner materials, metadata and future localization.  
**Canonical semantics:** NOVA's Terminology Governance Standard and Canonical Terminology Register, once approved and merged into the designated source-of-truth branch.

## 1. Purpose

Make AstroCrown's terminology understandable and consistent across products, repositories, public communications, collaborators and future languages. Brand consistency is not only consistent spelling or visual design: it requires consistent meanings, clear distinctions between concepts, traceable terminology decisions and evidence-based public claims.

This guide is the AstroCrown-Web application layer for the ecosystem-wide terminology system. It does not create a competing lexicon and must not silently redefine shared concepts owned by the NOVA canonical register.

## 2. Authority and hierarchy

Use the following source hierarchy:

1. **Canonical terminology register in NOVA:** shared concept IDs, preferred designations, definitions, typed relationships and term statuses.
2. **Approved project specifications or charters:** authoritative scope-specific attributes, technical behavior, token rights, governance procedures and product requirements.
3. **AstroCrown brand-language guidance:** audience-appropriate presentation and public-writing rules that do not change the concept's canonical meaning.
4. **Page, campaign or interface copy:** a contextual expression of the approved concept; it cannot override the sources above.
5. **Translation, partner wording or historical material:** a candidate or a context-specific variant until it has been checked against the canonical meaning and approved.

If two sources conflict, do not silently pick the version that sounds better. Record the conflict, identify the affected terms/pages and source revisions, mark the public-use decision BLOCKED where material, and obtain an authorized resolution.

## 3. Shared terminology and name usage

Use the preferred spelling, capitalization, acronym expansion and scope note from the canonical register. The following rules apply to current drafting, subject to the relevant source-of-truth changes being merged:

- **AstroCrown:** the brand/ecosystem name. Do not use the name alone as proof of a legal entity status, service availability, asset right or guarantee.
- **NOVA:** the evolving, AI-agnostic operational ecosystem and architectural framework. Do not revive an obsolete acronym expansion as the current definition.
- **HSIAN:** the adopted project term *Human–Super Intelligence Agentic Network*. Its public repository rollout is pending the HSIAN rename/integration pull requests; until they merge, existing main-branch files may still contain the previous designation.
- **Super Intelligence:** a project-defined term and capability horizon, not a certification that a particular current model meets an established scientific threshold for superintelligence.
- **Cognitive extension:** describes a functional or theoretical arrangement of person-plus-tools in a task-solving process; it does not automatically prove measured enhancement or neuroplasticity.
- **Cognitive prosthesis:** use only with the functional-analogy qualifier where needed. Do not imply a medical implant or literal neural merger unless separately evidenced.
- **ACROWN token:** use the canonical technical token name and applicable specification. Do not infer legal classification, asset backing, guaranteed reserves, investment rights or service availability from a brand name or ticker.
- **Imperial HSIAN / Imperial Council:** describe project-internal governance only within the defined charter. Do not imply external authority or powers beyond actual permissions, applicable law and consent.
- **RSI / Strong RSI:** describe capability and readiness according to current subsystem evidence. A research target or draft does not equal an operational or independently verified capability.

The full list is in the canonical register. When it differs from this short list, the register plus the applicable project specification controls.

## 4. Separate semantics from brand style

This guide distinguishes:
- **Concept semantics:** what a term means and how it relates to other concepts.
- **Brand style:** tone, readability, visual hierarchy, capitalization, punctuation and voice.
- **Technical specification:** how a product, system or interface actually behaves.
- **Public claim:** a statement of fact or outcome that needs substantiation.
- **Governance decision:** an authorized decision with a recorded scope and process.
- **Legal classification:** a conclusion that may depend on rights, implementation and jurisdiction.

Changing the tone of a sentence must not change the underlying technical meaning. A memorable tagline cannot replace the correct definition. A product's planned capability must not be expressed as a currently available feature.

## 5. Public claim controls

For material statements about features, performance, capacity, pricing, safety, privacy, security, standards, rights, governance or legal status:

1. Identify the exact claim and intended audience.
2. Link the claim to a current authoritative project source and, where needed, independent external evidence.
3. Verify status: implemented, tested, deployed, proposal, roadmap, prototype, candidate or blocked.
4. State scope, conditions, date/model/version or jurisdiction where these change the claim.
5. Avoid absolute or guaranteed language unless that exact guarantee is authorized and supported.
6. Preserve conflicting evidence and uncertainty instead of choosing only supportive sources.
7. Obtain the applicable product, security, privacy or legal review for high-impact claims.

A terminology entry establishes the preferred meaning of a concept, not proof that the product meets a requirement. An internal checklist does not prove certification. Never publish “ISO certified” or “certified by ISO”; ISO states that it does not perform certification or issue certificates.

For current terminology work, the accurate status statement is that the ecosystem is **establishing an internal system designed for alignment with the published scope of ISO 704:2022**. Do not call the system fully ISO 704:2022 conformant until the complete edition has been reviewed and the project has completed its controlled clause/topic-to-evidence matrix, operational evidence, internal audit and corrective-action review. **Possible independent certification remains a project objective, not a guarantee and not something to presume impossible. Provider discovery and accreditation checks are deliberately deferred until the internal readiness gate passes.** No conclusion is currently made about whether a suitable ISO 704:2022-specific certificate is available.

### Certification is a later, readiness-gated objective

Maintain a visible evidence trail toward a future independent assessment, but do not begin provider discovery, accreditation checks, enquiries or applications until the NOVA ISO 704:2022 Audit-Readiness Dossier has passed its mandatory GO gate. That dossier defines the scope freeze, controlled-standard prerequisite, clause/topic matrix, objective evidence, active-path sweep, operational records, internal audit, corrective-action retests and management decision.

After readiness has passed, any proposed provider's exact certificate, standard edition, scheme, criteria, issuer, assessed scope and assessment method must be established in writing. Verify accreditation by the relevant accreditation body for the actual activity and scope where accreditation is claimed. ISO itself does not issue certificates or accredit certification providers; do not call a body “ISO-approved” without specific evidence of the exact relationship.

Distinguish an accredited certificate, a non-accredited conformity statement, a readiness/gap assessment, consultancy, training and an audit report. They are not interchangeable. No public certification claim may be made until an actual issued certificate is validated and its scope checked. An internal readiness decision does not guarantee that a certificate can be obtained or that an external assessment will pass.

The readiness-first rule does not authorize any payment, standards purchase, application or paid tool. No financial commitment may be made without separate authorization.

## 6. Public accessibility and consistency

- Prefer the canonical term on first use, followed by its expansion/explanation where needed for a general audience.
- Define an unfamiliar concept in plain language without replacing the actual technical definition.
- Use the same name for the same concept across menus, buttons, product pages, help text, technical documentation, social posts and support responses.
- Do not reuse one UI label for multiple actions or concepts merely to save space if doing so would mislead users.
- A shorter variant may be used after the concept is clearly introduced and where ambiguity is unlikely.
- Maintain visible distinctions between a brand name, a product, a feature, a governance body, a token, a standard and a capability claim.
- Avoid language that implies operational availability when a feature is only a prototype or roadmap item.
- Review page titles, metadata, link labels, image alt text, captions, accessibility labels and machine-readable content when a term changes.

## 7. Localization and international consistency

English is the initial source language for this register. This does not mean every other language should receive a literal word-for-word translation.

For each localized term or key phrase:
- preserve the canonical concept, scope and relationships;
- identify language and locale separately where regional usage differs;
- record the preferred local designation, alternatives and usage notes;
- state whether the choice is a recognized domain term, literal translation, transliteration, transcreation or intentionally untranslated proper name;
- record the reviewer and validation status;
- preserve the original-language term if a reliable equivalent is not established, with a short explanation where appropriate;
- mark uncertain equivalence BLOCKED rather than inventing a translation;
- obtain qualified review for legal, financial, regulatory, privacy, security and safety language where jurisdiction matters.

A localized marketing expression can be adapted for culture and tone, but it cannot change token rights, technical behavior, governance powers, service commitments or the definition of a shared concept.

## 8. Naming and terminology change procedure

When proposing a new brand, product, feature, acronym or important concept:

1. Search the canonical register and active repositories for existing terms, synonyms, collisions and historical meanings.
2. Decide whether the proposed label denotes an existing concept or introduces a genuinely new one.
3. Document the intended meaning, domain, audience, essential distinction, neighboring terms and possible ambiguity.
4. Check spelling/pronunciation, acronym conflicts, cross-language risks, accessibility, searchability, third-party mark conflicts and legal sensitivities as appropriate.
5. Record candidate options and the reason for selecting one; separate subjective brand preference from factual evidence.
6. Obtain approval from the designated human maintainer or relevant authorized governance body.
7. Add/update the canonical entry first; then update dependent public guidance, interface strings, documentation, content and cross-references through reviewed repository changes.
8. Retain the previous term as deprecated with a successor pointer where history or searchability matters.
9. Run a bounded active-path search for missed usages and report the coverage.
10. Confirm no immutable backup/archive path was changed.

A term may be approved as a project name while associated scientific, performance, legal or operational claims remain unverified. Keep these decision states separate.

## 9. Roles and decision boundaries

- **Terminology steward:** maintains register structure, entry quality, links and change history.
- **Concept owner / domain reviewer:** checks whether the definition and relations match the actual technical/product/governance concept.
- **Public-content owner:** ensures pages, UI and communications use preferred terms without unsupported claims.
- **Localization reviewer:** checks concept-equivalence and locale-specific use.
- **Independent reviewer/auditor:** samples terms and actual use, verifies evidence and reports contradictions or gaps.

Until formal roles are assigned, the designated human maintainer retains approval authority. Models and collaborators may propose improvements but may not independently approve their own changes or suppress conflicting evidence. Any governance delegation must be recorded in an approved charter; this guide does not itself assign legal powers.

## 10. Audit checklist and required evidence

A review of AstroCrown terminology should record repository/ref/commit, paths and content sampled, dates, reviewers, source versions, result, findings and re-test.

- Are shared terms linked to a stable canonical entry?
- Does public copy match the current definition, scope and status?
- Are old/deprecated designations clearly distinguished from preferred current names?
- Are concepts confused with their designations, examples, slogans or legal classifications?
- Are technical features and performance claims substantiated against current implementations/tests?
- Are candidates, proposals, prototypes and operational capabilities clearly distinguished?
- Are translations reviewed for concept-equivalence rather than fluency alone?
- Are page UI labels, titles, metadata and accessibility labels consistent?
- Can each important term and claim be traced to its source or decision?
- Have conflicts and missing evidence been preserved as findings rather than hidden?
- Was the full changed-path list checked for backup/archive modifications?

Use the project's statuses PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED, N/A and CANDIDATE. Do not convert absence of evidence into PASS. Keep any standard clause not yet reviewed in the full ISO text visibly BLOCKED.

## 11. Boundaries and current status

This document is development-only guidance until reviewed and merged. It does not alter public root files or the website publication boundary. It does not change runtime code, product behavior, token economics, access control, security policy, deployment, or Strong RSI readiness.

The cross-repository terminology system is still in draft. The initial lexicon is not an exhaustive audit of every active repository path or every current public statement. Full ISO 704:2022 clause-level assessment, complete active-term sweep, localization work and independent review remain future gates.

## 12. Impact Analysis

**IF applied:** readers across public pages, documentation, products and future languages will encounter consistent terms; project-defined names stay separate from technical facts and legal classifications; and an auditor can trace public wording to a canonical definition and source.

**IF not applied:** inconsistent naming and loosely controlled claims may reappear in user interfaces, social posts, service descriptions and translations even when the canonical register exists.
