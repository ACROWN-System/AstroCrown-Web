01 · Foundations

Privacy words do not all mean the same thing

A system can protect account credentials while its blockchain transactions remain public. It can use pseudonyms without making users anonymous. Clear definitions prevent false confidence.

TermWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
PrivacyControl over information: what is collected, used, linked, stored and disclosed.That no one can ever infer anything about you.
ConfidentialityInformation is restricted to authorized parties or protected from unauthorized disclosure.That the information does not exist or cannot be disclosed under any circumstances.
PseudonymityYou interact through an identifier that does not directly state your legal name.That activity cannot be linked to you through other evidence.
AnonymityYour activity cannot reasonably be attributed to you within a defined threat model.A universal status that can be guaranteed by a wallet, VPN or blockchain.
AuthenticationA method establishes control of an account or authenticator.Proof of legal identity, unique personhood, wallet ownership in every context, or transaction privacy.
Self-custodyYou control the private keys that authorize your wallet's actions.That your address, balances or transactions are hidden from a public ledger.

The distinction to remember: account security protects access; custody concerns control of assets; privacy concerns information exposure. They are related, but none automatically guarantees the others.

02 · Public ledgers

What a public blockchain can reveal

On a transparent public network, anyone may inspect the ledger without being given permission by AstroCrown. Depending on the network and transaction type, public information can include:

Addresses and relationships

Wallet addresses, token accounts, contract addresses, transaction participants, repeated counterparties and patterns of interaction.

Balances and movements

Token holdings, transfer amounts, deposits, withdrawals, fees and changes in balances where the protocol exposes them.

Timing and transaction detail

Block or transaction time, ordering, network, token or mint, contract calls, public events and transaction status.

Links created outside the chain

Public posts, websites, screenshots, donation announcements or other disclosures that associate an address with a person or pseudonym.

A wallet address is usually a pseudonymous identifier—not a legal name. But funding patterns, reused addresses, exchange records, public statements and timing correlations may allow an observer to associate addresses with people or organisations.

Important for supporters: sending a contribution to a public receiving address may expose the sending address, amount, token, destination and transaction time. AstroCrown not publishing your name does not make the payment private.

“USDT” is not a complete network description

The same token name can exist on different blockchains, with different token contracts, mints, fees and wallet requirements. Tether publishes protocol-specific USD₮ identifiers and explicitly distinguishes currently supported protocols from historical or deprecated ones. Always check the exact network and token identifier, not just the ticker symbol. See Tether's supported protocols and token identifiers.

AstroCrown's future payment interface must display the selected network and asset explicitly—for example, USD₮ · Ethereum · ERC-20 or USD₮ · Solana · SPL—and verify the precise token identifier and receiving route before accepting payment.

03 · Infrastructure visibility

Privacy exposure can happen before a transaction is sent

Blockchain privacy is not only about the final transaction. The software and infrastructure used to check balances, search addresses, estimate fees or prepare a transaction can also observe information.

Service or layerWhat it may observeWhat determines the exposure
Wallet applicationAddresses it manages, connected sites, local activity and the details required to prepare signatures.Wallet architecture, permissions, telemetry and privacy settings.
dApp / websiteA connected public address, requested signatures, user actions and data deliberately entered or shared.Connection flow, requested permissions, scripts, logs and what the user approves.
RPC / node providerNetwork requests, queried addresses or contracts, timing, request volume and potentially source IP or device/network metadata.Direct versus shared RPC, proxying, provider logging and its privacy policy.
Indexers / analyticsPublic transaction history, address relationships, token movements and derived behavioural patterns.Public data access, indexing scope, enrichment sources and the provider's own records.
Hosting / security providerRequests, IP addresses, timestamps, security events and operational metadata.Application design, logs, retention, hosting configuration and provider terms.
AI inference providerPrompts, documents, outputs, account metadata and usage information sent through its endpoint.Exact provider, model, API route, plan, data controls and current terms.

Ethereum's current privacy roadmap describes how ordinary RPC requests can expose IP addresses, device fingerprints, queried addresses and activity timing to node providers. The exact exposure depends on the wallet and network architecture. Read the Ethereum privacy roadmap.

One provider's policy is not another provider's policy. A free plan may have different data-use rules from a paid plan. A gateway may pass requests to another provider. AstroCrown must qualify the exact endpoint and route before sending private, confidential or personal information to it.

AI-service example: free does not automatically mean private

Google's current Gemini API additional terms say content submitted to unpaid services may be used to improve Google products and may be reviewed by humans. The terms advise users not to submit sensitive, confidential or personal information to unpaid services. This is a rule for that service and plan—not a claim about every AI provider. Read the Gemini API Additional Terms.

Groq separately documents its inference-data retention controls, including limited retention for reliability and abuse investigations, and an optional Zero Data Retention setting. The applicable route and account settings must be checked rather than inferred from the provider name. Read Groq's data-retention documentation.

04 · AstroCrown design commitments

What AstroCrown is designed to protect

Protection must be built into the architecture and verified. It must not be a privilege that depends on a user's profession, political circumstances or geographic identity.

Collect the minimum necessary information

Every data field must have a defined purpose, access rule, retention period and deletion or archival decision. Do not collect identity information simply because it might be useful later.

Keep account identity separate from wallet activity

Account, pseudonym/profile, authenticator, wallet, blockchain address, payment record and eligibility decision are different objects. Do not link them by default without a necessary, disclosed reason.

Keep secrets off public networks

Never place seed phrases, private keys, passkey secrets, recovery material, identity documents or private user memory on a public blockchain. A server should not ask a user to submit their wallet seed phrase or private key.

Make disclosures before consequential actions

Before a future wallet connection, public post or payment, explain what is shared, which network and token are involved, what will become public, and which third parties process the request.

Use proportionate, explainable access controls

Do not treat a VPN, Tor, shared network, multiple devices or a privacy-conscious browser as proof of wrongdoing. Investigate a specific action with evidence; avoid blanket exclusion where a narrower control is available.

Protect people in sensitive circumstances

Journalists, human-rights defenders, whistleblowers, investigators, judges and other people facing heightened risks should not be excluded merely because of their profession or viewpoint. Avoid unnecessary identity or financial exposure and explain residual risks honestly.

Verify every third-party data route

Review the exact provider, model, endpoint, terms, retention, training use, region, quotas and account settings. Unknown data handling is a blocker for sensitive data, not an assumed approval.

Do not promise absolute anonymity

Explain the threat model and limits. Privacy tools can reduce particular exposures; they do not eliminate all on-chain, network, device, provider or human disclosure risks.

These are architectural and product requirements. They must not be read as a statement that every control has already been implemented or independently verified.

Where a mandatory requirement genuinely applies to a particular service, provider, entity or transaction, AstroCrown must identify that specific constraint and its scope. A selectable privacy-oriented or global-facing experience must not be used to evade a rule that actually applies. At the same time, a user's country, profession, political view or risk profile must not be used as an unsupported substitute for a specific assessment.

For people facing heightened risk: this current AstroCrown preview is not a production-secure environment and should not be used to coordinate sensitive operations or expose real asset details. The design requirement is to provide strong, broadly available protection without selective exclusion; that requirement is not a claim that those protections are already deployed or independently assessed.

05 · Learn before you act

Common privacy misconceptions—and the reality

These misunderstandings are common enough, and consequential enough, that we should correct them at the point where they affect a user's decision.

“Crypto is anonymous.”

Most transparent public blockchains are pseudonymous. Addresses are not normally labelled with legal names, but transaction graphs, exchange records, public disclosures and other data can make activity attributable. Ethereum's privacy overview explains the difference.

“No KYC means nobody can identify me.”

Not collecting identity documents does not hide a public wallet address, transaction history, IP address or third-party records. It can reduce one form of identity collection without guaranteeing anonymity.

“A self-custody wallet makes my payments private.”

Self-custody means you control the signing keys. On a transparent chain, the address and transaction details may still be visible to the public. Key control and transaction confidentiality solve different problems.

“A passkey or encrypted account hides my blockchain activity.”

A passkey protects authentication. Encryption protects specific information in specific places. Neither removes a transaction already recorded on a public chain nor conceals an address that is publicly associated with a profile.

“A new wallet address guarantees a clean break from old activity.”

A new address may reduce direct address reuse, but funding sources, counterparties, transaction patterns, timing, device or provider metadata and public disclosures may still link activity. Address separation is not a guarantee.

“A VPN or Tor makes all activity anonymous.”

These tools can reduce exposure of some network identifiers to some observers. They do not erase public-chain records or prevent every form of browser, wallet, account, timing or behavioural correlation. Do not rely on them as a complete anonymity system.

“Zero-knowledge proofs make the whole application private.”

A zero-knowledge proof can reveal only a specific verified statement instead of the underlying information. But the surrounding application may still expose addresses, timing, network requests, public inputs or user actions. Privacy must be assessed end to end.

“A token with confidential transfers hides every detail.”

It depends on the exact token, mint, account setup and transfer method. Solana's Confidential Balances feature encrypts transfer amounts and balances for supported Token-2022 mints, while account addresses, mint, owners and transaction participation remain public. An optional auditor key may also decrypt transfer amounts. This is not the default for every SPL token. See Solana's privacy comparison.

“Deleting my AstroCrown account erases my blockchain history.”

Off-chain data may be deleted or restricted according to the applicable policy and law. A confirmed public-chain transaction is generally not something AstroCrown can delete, rewrite or remove from independent copies and indexes.

“Every USDT token and network is interchangeable for a payment.”

The token name alone is not enough. A receiving system must support the exact blockchain and token identifier. Sending to the wrong network or contract can result in an unrecoverable loss. Always compare the complete asset-and-network label with the wallet confirmation.

“Stablecoins have the same privacy and control properties as cash.”

A stablecoin is a token issued under specific terms and contracts, not physical cash. Issuer policies, smart-contract design, public-chain visibility, wallet providers and applicable law all matter. Read the actual issuer terms for the exact asset; do not generalize one issuer's powers to every token.

“A stablecoin issuer can never affect tokens held in my self-custody wallet.”

Self-custody means you control the wallet's private keys; it does not remove controls built into an issuer's token or the issuer's contractual powers. For example, Tether's Terms of Sale and Service, last updated 26 February 2026, reserve powers that include freezing Tether Tokens, blacklisting digital-token addresses and suspending services in circumstances described in those terms. This is specific to Tether's products and terms; do not generalize it to every stablecoin. Read Tether's current legal terms.

06 · Emerging developments

New privacy technology is useful—but not magic

Solana Confidential Balances

Solana's Confidential Balances is an optional Token-2022 extension. A mint must be created with the extension and accounts must be configured. The transfer amount and confidential balance can be encrypted, while the mint, token accounts, owners and transaction participation remain public. A mint may also configure an auditor key that can decrypt transfer amounts. Read the technical documentation.

AstroCrown does not claim that ACROWN or USD₮ transfers are confidential. No privacy feature is inherited automatically from the Solana network. Any future integration would require the exact mint, token program, wallet support, auditor model, compatibility and user experience to be independently qualified.

Ethereum private reads, private writes and private proving

Ethereum's current privacy roadmap separates three problems: hiding the data a wallet reads, protecting the privacy of submitted transactions, and proving facts without revealing all underlying personal information. The roadmap describes work in progress; it is not a guarantee that ordinary Ethereum wallets or transactions already provide these protections by default. Review the current roadmap and its status.

Zero-knowledge and selective disclosure

These techniques can help a person prove a narrowly defined fact—such as meeting an eligibility condition—without publishing all underlying details. The privacy claim must still state exactly what is proven, who verifies it, what metadata is visible, whether the proof can be linked across uses, and what happens if a verifier or issuer retains additional data.

Threat models must keep evolving

Public ledgers persist, and analysis tools can improve. Information that is difficult to associate with a person today may become easier to correlate later. Privacy guidance therefore needs dated sources, recurring review and correction when technology or provider practices change.

08 · Practical safety

Before you connect, send or publish

Use this checklist whenever an application asks you to connect a wallet, sign a message, submit a transaction or associate an address with a public profile.

  • Check the destination. Verify the website address and the application you intended to open. A familiar logo is not proof that a site is authentic.
  • Understand the connection. A connected application may learn your public wallet address. Read the requested permissions and disconnect unused sessions.
  • Read every signature request. A message signature and a blockchain transaction are not the same action. Do not sign a request you cannot understand.
  • Verify asset and network together. Confirm token, chain, contract or mint, destination, amount and fee before confirming a payment.
  • Assume a public transaction can be analysed. Consider whether the amount, timing, source address or destination could expose information you did not intend to share.
  • Keep recovery secrets private. Never send seed phrases, private keys or offline recovery secrets to a person, website, support agent or chat.
  • Think before linking an address to a profile. Once a public address is associated with a name or pseudonym, copies, indexes and past transactions may preserve that link.
  • Use only providers suitable for the information. Do not submit personal, confidential or sensitive information to a free AI, RPC or analytics route unless its exact handling terms have been reviewed and approved.

If a screen does not clearly identify what will be public or which network and asset are being used, stop and seek clarification before proceeding.
